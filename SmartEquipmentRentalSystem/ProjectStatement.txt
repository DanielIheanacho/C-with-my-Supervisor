Project: Smart Equipment Rental System
Real-world scenario

A small engineering company rents out equipment to customers.

The company owns different types of equipment, such as:

Drills
Welding machines
Generators
Air compressors
Measuring instruments
Ladders
Power tools

Customers can rent equipment for a specified number of days. However, the company needs a system to keep track of its equipment and rentals.

Your task is to design and implement the system using OOP.

What the system should be able to do

The company should be able to:

Register new equipment.
Register customers.
Display all available equipment.
Display equipment currently rented out.
Allow a customer to rent equipment.
Prevent a customer from renting equipment that is already rented.
Record when equipment was rented.
Record the expected return date.
Return rented equipment.
Calculate the rental cost.
Identify overdue equipment.
Calculate an additional charge when equipment is returned late.
Display a customer's current rentals.
Display the rental history of a customer.
Display the rental history of a particular piece of equipment.
Allow equipment to be marked as unavailable when it requires maintenance.
Prevent unavailable equipment from being rented.
Allow equipment to be returned to service after maintenance.

Important business rules

Here are some rules the company has decided on.

Equipment

Every piece of equipment has:

A unique identification number.
A name.
A category.
A daily rental price.
A current condition.
A status.

Equipment can be in different states, such as:

Available → Rented → Available

or

Available → Maintenance → Available

You decide exactly how you want to model these states.

Customers

A customer has information such as:

Identification number
Name
Phone number
Address

A customer can have multiple rentals.

However, the company doesn't want customers accumulating an unreasonable number of active rentals.

You must decide how this restriction should work.

Rentals

A rental represents the relationship between:

Customer + Equipment + Rental period

For example:

Daniel rents Generator G001 for 5 days.

The rental should keep enough information to determine:

When the rental started.
When it should end.
Whether it has been returned.
How much it should cost.
Whether it is overdue.
Whether an additional charge is required.
Different equipment behaves differently

Here's where the project becomes more interesting.

Not every piece of equipment should necessarily be charged in exactly the same way.

For example:

Standard equipment

Normal calculation:

Daily price × number of days

Heavy equipment

Heavy equipment could have an additional fixed service charge.

Measuring equipment

Measuring instruments might have a calibration requirement.

For example, the company may require a measuring instrument to be inspected after a certain number of rentals.

You should decide how your design represents these differences.

Don't simply put a huge if/else statement inside your rental system.

Think about what OOP principle could help you model this.

Maintenance

Equipment sometimes develops problems.

For example:

Generator G001 is returned with a damaged cable.

The employee can place the generator into maintenance.

While equipment is under maintenance:

It cannot be rented.
It should not appear among available equipment.
Someone should be able to record why it was sent for maintenance.
It should eventually be returned to service.

You decide how maintenance records should work.

Console application

For now, make this a console application.

Something roughly like:

===== EQUIPMENT RENTAL SYSTEM =====

1. Register Equipment
2. Register Customer
3. View Equipment
4. View Customers
5. Rent Equipment
6. Return Equipment
7. View Customer Rentals
8. View Rental History
9. Send Equipment for Maintenance
10. Return Equipment to Service
11. View Overdue Rentals
12. Exit

Select an option:

The exact menu and user interface are entirely up to you.

Constraints

I deliberately want you to work within these constraints:

You may use
Classes
Objects
Interfaces
Abstract classes
Inheritance
Encapsulation
Properties
Constructors
Methods
Enums
List<T>
Loops
Conditional statements
Exception handling
Basic LINQ if you've learned it


Don't use
Dictionary<TKey,TValue>
HashSet<T>
Hash tables
Databases
Entity Framework
External APIs
UI frameworks

For persistence, just keep everything in memory while the program is running.
